Sarflog architecture lesson 002

Simple vs compound: which balance gets multiplied next?

Forget the big formula for a moment. Use a deliberately easy example: borrow 1,000 UZS at 10% per month and make no payments for two months.

The entire difference: simple interest multiplies the rate by principal only. Compound interest multiplies the rate by principal plus earlier unpaid interest.

Simple interest: earlier interest stays separate

MomentBalance usedCalculationNew interestTotal owed

Point to the exact difference

Simple

month 2: 1,000 × 10% = 100

The earlier 100 remains interest. It does not become principal.

Compound

month 2: 1,100 × 10% = 110

The earlier 100 joined the interest-bearing balance.

Difference

1,210 − 1,200 = 10

That extra 10 is interest charged on earlier interest.

What a normal amortized payment does

Now start again with 1,000 principal and 10% monthly interest, but make a 600 payment after month one.

StepCalculationResult
Month 1 interest1,000 × 10%100 interest
Payment covers interest first600 − 100500 left for principal

The month-one interest was fully paid, so no interest remained to join the balance. Sarflog's planned STANDARD_AMORTIZATION follows this pattern: each scheduled payment covers that period's interest and then reduces principal.

Where Sarflog draws the line

Agreement behaviorSarflog v1 treatment

Do not ask users to guess “simple or compound”

Sarflog should disclose its narrow supported rule. If the provider agreement behaves differently, copy the provider schedule instead of producing confident but false mathematics.

Ten-second self-check

A 1,000 balance earns 10% interest. After the first period it is 1,100. Next period's interest is calculated as 110. Did earlier interest join the interest-bearing balance?

Answer: yes. This is compound behavior because 10% was applied to 1,100, not the original 1,000.
