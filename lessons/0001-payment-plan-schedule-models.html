Sarflog architecture lesson 001

Three schedule models, not three kinds of reality

A product says what the agreement is. A schedule model says how Sarflog obtains the dated amounts. Keeping those axes separate is the foundation of a durable rebuild.

Verdict: fixed total, standard amortization, and exact contract rows are a strong first set for closed-end obligations. They are not a universal model of every credit product, and they should never be presented as one.

The three strategies

1. Fixed total

The provider has already told the customer the final amount owed. Sarflog divides the unpaid amount into installments.

unpaid = final total − upfront paid
row ≈ unpaid ÷ count

Reality: pay-in-four BNPL, store installments, a fixed-price service paid over time.

Unknown: no interest calculation is needed; any markup may already be baked into the quoted total.

2. Standard amortization

The customer borrowed principal. Each payment contains interest on the declining principal plus some principal repayment.

interest = remaining principal × period rate
principal part = payment − interest

Reality: many fixed-rate mortgages, auto loans, and personal loans.

Movement: early payments are interest-heavy; later payments are principal-heavy.

3. Exact contract rows

Sarflog does not invent the math. It records the dates and components shown by the authoritative agreement or lender.

schedule = the contract's exact rows

Reality: balloon payments, irregular milestones, negotiated repayment agreements, lender-specific fees or rounding.

Important: this is an input strategy, not a financial product.

Where the first version must stop

Do not fake these with a standard generator

Revolving credit cards, redrawable credit lines, adjustable-rate resets, income-driven repayments, negative amortization, and daily-interest recasting have rules that react to future events. Exact rows can record a lender's current schedule, but they do not turn Sarflog into the lender's live calculation engine.

The production rule

Generate when the rule is supported. Copy exact rows when the contract is more complex. Materialize both outcomes into the same schedule-row structure so payments, charges, write-offs, history, and reversals do not care how the original rows were created.

Primary reading: CFPB on amortization. Project decision: ADR 0028. Keep asking follow-up questions—especially by giving a real contract example and asking which strategy it belongs to.
